## Current behaviour

The defect described in `REV-20260805-009` is still present. 3 fixes were written and each was rejected on verification.

## Proposed behaviour

Decide how to proceed: accept a narrower fix that covers the common case and say so explicitly, change the data model so the case can be represented, or close the finding as won't-fix.

## Alternatives

**Attempt 1** (`482d5fc88`) was rejected:

`today` comes from `toISOString()` (UTC), while `nextPaymentAfterToday` parses dates as local calendar dates. At 8 p.m. EST on March 4, 2026, `today` becomes `2026-03-05`. For a monthly loan due March 5, the loop treats that still-future local payment as already due and projects April 5 first. A rate change effective March 20 is then applied to the first projected payment even though the actual March 5 payment should use the old rate.

**Attempt 2** (`ef16f93d9`) was rejected:

addMonthsClamped derives the next day from the previously clamped occurrence rather than the configured payment-start day. A monthly loan starting January 31 becomes February 28 and then March 28 permanently. For example, on March 5, 2026, a loan anchored to January 31 projects March 28 instead of the actual March 31 due date. A rate change effective March 29 is therefore applied only to the following projected installment even though it should apply to the March 31 payment.

**Attempt 3** (`fe5177e97`) was rejected:

Only `firstPaymentDate` is derived from `paymentStartDate`; the schedule then advances that date with `advanceDate`, which uses `Date.setMonth` without preserving the original anchor. For a monthly loan anchored January 31 and projected on March 5, the first row is correctly March 31, but advancing it produces May 1 instead of April 30. A rate change effective May 1 is therefore applied to the second projected installment even though the real April 30 installment should use the old rate. The added tests inspect only `firstPaymentDate`, so they do not catch the subsequent drift.
