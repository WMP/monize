## Location

`frontend/src/lib/loan-history.ts:367`

## Failure scenario

buildLoanProjectionInput sets the first projected payment to one frequency interval after today rather than advancing from paymentStartDate. On August 5 for a monthly loan due on the first, it projects September 5 instead of September 1. A rate change effective September 3 is consequently applied to the first payment even though the real September 1 payment should use the previous rate.

## Recommended fix

Derive the next payment date by advancing the configured payment-start/due-date cadence until it is after today, using date-only arithmetic that clamps month ends.

## Re-review of `482d5fc88db2`

Not fixed. `today` comes from `toISOString()` (UTC), while `nextPaymentAfterToday` parses dates as local calendar dates. At 8 p.m. EST on March 4, 2026, `today` becomes `2026-03-05`. For a monthly loan due March 5, the loop treats that still-future local payment as already due and projects April 5 first. A rate change effective March 20 is then applied to the first projected payment even though the actual March 5 payment should use the old rate.


## Re-review of `ef16f93d9f43`

Not fixed. addMonthsClamped derives the next day from the previously clamped occurrence rather than the configured payment-start day. A monthly loan starting January 31 becomes February 28 and then March 28 permanently. For example, on March 5, 2026, a loan anchored to January 31 projects March 28 instead of the actual March 31 due date. A rate change effective March 29 is therefore applied only to the following projected installment even though it should apply to the March 31 payment.


## Re-review of `fe5177e97026`

Not fixed. Only `firstPaymentDate` is derived from `paymentStartDate`; the schedule then advances that date with `advanceDate`, which uses `Date.setMonth` without preserving the original anchor. For a monthly loan anchored January 31 and projected on March 5, the first row is correctly March 31, but advancing it produces May 1 instead of April 30. A rate change effective May 1 is therefore applied to the second projected installment even though the real April 30 installment should use the old rate. The added tests inspect only `firstPaymentDate`, so they do not catch the subsequent drift.
